Poker Hand Rankings

From strongest to weakest. A higher hand on this list always beats a lower one. The same list is used in both Five Card Draw and Texas Hold'em.

When two players have the same kind of hand

  • Higher cards win. A pair of Kings beats a pair of Nines. A Flush with an Ace beats a Flush topped by a King.
  • Kickers. If the main part is equal (both have a pair of Jacks), the remaining cards are compared from highest down. That leftover card is called the kicker.
  • Full House: the three-of-a-kind part is compared first, then the pair.
  • Two Pair: top pair first, then the second pair, then the kicker.
  • Suits never matter. ♠︎ ♥︎ ♦︎ ♣︎ are all equal. If hands are exactly equal, the pot is split.
  • A-2-3-4-5 is the lowest straight (a "wheel"). The Ace can be high or low, but you can't wrap around (Q-K-A-2-3 is not a straight).

The two games

Five Card Draw

Everyone gets 5 private cards. After a betting round, you may swap up to 3 cards (4 if you keep an Ace). Then there is one more betting round and a showdown. Your hand is just your 5 cards.

Texas Hold'em

Everyone gets 2 private cards. Then 5 shared cards are dealt face up in the middle: the flop (3), the turn (1) and the river (1), with betting between each. Your hand is the best 5 cards from your 2 plus the 5 shared ones.

Betting basics

  • Blinds: the two players left of the dealer button (D) put in $10 and $20 before the cards come, so there's always something to play for.
  • Check: pass without betting, only if nobody has bet yet. Call: match the current bet. Raise: bet more. Fold: give up your hand.
  • All-in: if you bet all your chips, you can only win as much from each player as you put in yourself. Any extra goes to a side pot.

Fair shuffle

Each hand starts from a fresh 52-card deck, shuffled with the Fisher–Yates method and your device's cryptographic random number generator (the same kind used for security). Every possible order of the deck is equally likely, just like a perfect real-life shuffle. Nothing is weighted, nobody gets "lucky streaks" on purpose, and the computer players can't see your cards or the deck.

Blackjack Rules

Beat the dealer by getting closer to 21 than they do, without going over.

What the cards are worth

Examples

Your moves

  • Hit: take another card.
  • Stand: keep your total and end your turn.
  • Double: on your first two cards only. Double your bet and take exactly one more card.
  • Split: if your first two cards have the same value, split them into two separate hands, each with its own bet (up to 4 hands). Split Aces get one card each, and 21 after a split pays 1 to 1, not 3 to 2.

How the dealer plays

  • One of the dealer's cards stays face down until you finish.
  • With an Ace or a 10-value card showing, the dealer checks for Blackjack first. If they have it, the round ends at once.
  • The dealer has no choices: they must draw on 16 or less and stand on every 17, soft 17 included. That's the "Dealer must draw to 16 and stand on all 17's" printed on the table.

Payouts

  • Win: 1 to 1 (bet $50, win $50).
  • Blackjack: 3 to 2 (bet $50, win $75).
  • Push (same total as the dealer): your bet comes back.
  • Bust (over 21): you lose straight away, even if the dealer busts later.

Fair shuffle

The dealer uses a 6-deck shoe (312 cards) shuffled with the Fisher–Yates method and your device's cryptographic random number generator. Every order is equally likely and nothing is weighted. A cut card is placed 1 to 1.5 decks from the back, and when it comes out a fresh shoe is shuffled, just like in a casino.
